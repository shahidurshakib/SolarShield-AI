## 1. Project Impact Overview

SolarShield-AI aims to bridge the gap between solar activity analysis and Earth-level preparedness by developing an explainable, AI-driven space weather risk assessment and early-warning framework.

Solar activity continuously changes, producing phenomena such as solar flares and coronal mass ejections (CMEs) that can influence Earth's space environment and technological infrastructure. These disturbances can pose risks to satellites, navigation systems, aviation, communication networks, and electrical power infrastructure.

However, understanding solar activity alone is not sufficient. Decision-makers also need to understand what these changes could mean for Earth, which technological sectors may be affected, when potential impacts could occur, and what preparation measures may be appropriate.

SolarShield-AI addresses this broader challenge through a research-driven framework that connects:

**Solar Activity → Space Weather → Earth Response → Risk Assessment → Early Warning → Mitigation**

Its long-term impact vision is to transform complex solar and space weather information into understandable, explainable, and actionable insights for researchers, infrastructure operators, policymakers, and communities.

## 2. The Core Impact

SolarShield-AI focuses on six major impact dimensions.

### 2.1 Scientific Impact

The project contributes to solar-cycle research and data-driven understanding of long-term solar activity.

Key contributions include:

- Historical analysis of International Sunspot Number data.
- Investigation of solar activity patterns across Solar Cycles 1–25.
- Statistical identification of long-term trends using methods such as the Mann–Kendall trend test.
- Investigation of solar-cycle amplitude, duration, phase, and variability.
- Development and comparison of cycle-conditioned forecasting approaches.
- Exploration of explainable AI methods for understanding model behaviour.
- Quantitative evaluation through MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and R² where appropriate.
- Investigation of forecast uncertainty and model reliability.

The project aims to contribute reproducible research that helps researchers investigate solar variability and its potential implications for the Earth system.

### 2.2 Technological Impact

SolarShield-AI proposes an integrated analytical framework that combines historical solar observations, statistical analysis, machine learning, and explainability.

Its intended technological contributions include:

- Solar activity monitoring and historical data analysis.
- Solar-cycle forecasting research.
- Space weather information integration.
- Sector-oriented risk assessment.
- Probabilistic risk communication.
- Explainable alert generation.
- Forecast uncertainty visualization.
- Decision-support and mitigation recommendation interfaces.

The long-term objective is to make complex space weather information easier to interpret and use.

**Important:** The current research and model-development results do not establish an operational, validated Earth-impact forecasting service. Impact-specific prediction and warning capabilities require additional data, independent validation, and real-world testing.

### 2.3 Societal Impact

Modern societies increasingly depend on technologies that can be affected by severe space weather.

SolarShield-AI seeks to improve awareness and preparedness by making space weather information more accessible and understandable.

Potential societal benefits include:

- Improving public understanding of space weather risks.
- Supporting awareness of technological dependencies on space infrastructure.
- Helping educational institutions introduce solar and space weather research.
- Supporting informed preparedness discussions among relevant stakeholders.
- Encouraging collaboration between researchers, engineers, and infrastructure planners.

The project emphasizes preparedness rather than creating unnecessary public alarm.

### 2.4 Infrastructure and Economic Impact

Space weather disturbances can create operational challenges for critical technological infrastructure.

SolarShield-AI identifies several sectors where improved space weather awareness could have practical value.

| Sector | Potential impact | Intended contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Satellites | Radiation exposure, charging, and orbital drag during relevant space weather conditions | Risk awareness and preparedness insights |
| GPS/GNSS | Navigation and positioning inaccuracies | Space weather awareness for navigation-dependent services |
| Aviation | Communication and navigation disruptions, particularly on polar routes | Operational risk information |
| Telecommunications | Disturbances affecting radio communication | Communication-risk awareness |
| Power grids | Geomagnetically induced currents and potential transformer stress | Grid preparedness and monitoring support |
| Research and education | Limited accessibility of integrated solar activity analysis | Open academic research and visualization |

The potential economic impact includes reducing avoidable operational disruption, improving preparedness planning, and supporting more informed infrastructure management.

These benefits are prospective. Actual economic savings or reductions in infrastructure failures have not yet been demonstrated by SolarShield-AI.

### 2.5 Environmental and Earth System Impact

The project aligns with the Earth System Trend Detective challenge by investigating the relationship between changing solar activity and the broader Earth-space environment.

Its research direction includes:

- Identifying long-term solar activity patterns.
- Distinguishing statistically meaningful trends from short-term variability.
- Investigating the relationship between solar activity and space weather conditions.
- Exploring the implications of space weather for technological systems on Earth.
- Supporting scientific interpretation through transparent analytical methods.

SolarShield-AI does not assume that solar variability explains all observed Earth system changes. Its scientific approach distinguishes observed evidence, model interpretation, and potential operational implications.

### 2.6 Educational and Research Impact

SolarShield-AI also aims to make solar and space weather research more accessible to students and early-career researchers.

Planned contributions include:

- Free academic access to selected research tools and visualizations.
- Educational resources explaining solar cycles and space weather.
- Historical solar activity datasets and analytical demonstrations.
- Explainable forecasting research outputs.
- Opportunities for interdisciplinary student collaboration.
- Reproducible research workflows and documentation.

This approach can help expand participation in space science and AI-driven Earth system research, particularly in developing research communities.

## 5. Research-Driven Impact and Evidence

SolarShield-AI's impact depends on scientific credibility, reproducibility, and transparent model evaluation.

### 5.1 Historical Solar Data

The primary research foundation is the SILSO International Sunspot Number monthly dataset.

The research dataset contains:

- Historical monthly observations beginning in January 1749.
- Completed Solar Cycles 1–24.
- The ongoing Solar Cycle 25.
- Provisional observations for the latest months, subject to source updates.

Historical solar-cycle analysis provides a foundation for investigating recurring patterns, variability, and forecasting approaches.

### 5.2 Model Development

The project has investigated multiple approaches rather than assuming that a single forecasting method is sufficient.

Research directions include:

- Historical statistical baselines.
- Phase-based solar-cycle reconstruction.
- Constrained spline-based modelling.
- Amplitude-aware forecasting.
- Cycle-conditioned forecasting.
- Closed-loop and open-loop forecasting comparisons.
- Ensemble and data-assimilation research directions.

### 5.3 Validation and Scientific Integrity

Model evaluation emphasizes:

- Historical out-of-sample evaluation.
- Chronological separation of development and evaluation data.
- Fold-level performance assessment.
- Error attribution and diagnostic analysis.
- Forecast reconstruction checks.
- Reproducibility and source integrity.
- Explicit reporting of unsuccessful experiments.

The V2 research experiments demonstrated that model complexity does not automatically produce better forecasting performance. The tested amplitude-aware candidates did not meet the predefined primary acceptance thresholds.

The V3 research direction explores adaptive cycle-conditioned forecasting and data assimilation, with separate configuration selection, calibration, and final historical evaluation stages.

Protocol and source-verification checks are important milestones, but they must not be confused with successful forecast calibration or independent validation.

**Scientific principle:** A model result is reported as successful only when the corresponding experiment has been executed, independently checked against its protocol, and met its predefined acceptance criteria.

## 6. Impact Through Explainability and Trust

SolarShield-AI aims to make predictive information understandable rather than presenting users with unexplained risk scores.

The intended framework incorporates:

**Explainable AI**

SHAP-based explanations can help identify which model inputs contribute to a prediction, subject to the model and data used.

**Probabilistic Communication**

Risk information should communicate probabilities and forecast ranges rather than presenting uncertain outcomes as guaranteed events.

**Uncertainty Awareness**

Forecast uncertainty should remain visible throughout the decision-support process.

**Transparent Evidence**

Every scientific claim should be distinguishable as an observation, statistical result, model interpretation, or proposed operational decision.

**Responsible Alerts**

An alert should explain its risk level, relevant time window, confidence or probability where validated, and the factors supporting the assessment.

These principles are intended to improve scientific transparency and responsible use.

## 8. From Research to Real-World Impact

SolarShield-AI follows a staged development pathway.

### Stage 1 — Historical Research and Data Foundation

Establish reliable historical solar datasets, investigate long-term trends, and develop reproducible analytical workflows.

### Stage 2 — Model Development and Evaluation

Develop and compare forecasting approaches through controlled experiments, historical backtesting, and uncertainty analysis.

### Stage 3 — Space Weather Integration

Integrate relevant space weather observations and investigate the relationship between solar activity and geospace conditions.

### Stage 4 — Earth-Level Risk Research

Develop and independently evaluate sector-specific impact indicators using appropriate physical observations and validated impact data.

### Stage 5 — Explainable Decision-Support Prototype

Develop an interface that presents research outputs, uncertainty, risk information, and relevant explanatory factors.

### Stage 6 — Shadow-Mode Pilot

Work with suitable research or infrastructure partners to evaluate the system without allowing unvalidated outputs to control operational decisions.

### Stage 7 — Academic and Professional Deployment

Expand academic access, establish research collaborations, and investigate professional and enterprise applications following successful validation.

## 10. Responsible Innovation and Limitations

SolarShield-AI recognizes that space weather forecasting and Earth-impact assessment involve substantial scientific uncertainty.

The project therefore commits to the following principles:

- No guarantee of predicting every solar storm.
- No claim of operational readiness without independent validation.
- No replacement of official space weather monitoring and forecasting authorities.
- No unsupported claims of preventing satellite failures or power outages.
- No confusion between solar activity forecasting and geomagnetic impact prediction.
- No presentation of model uncertainty as absolute certainty.
- No use of unvalidated risk estimates as authoritative operational instructions.

Solar activity, interplanetary conditions, geomagnetic responses, and technological impacts are related but distinct stages. Each requires suitable observations, physical understanding, and independent evaluation.

The project's impact will ultimately depend on whether its research outputs can be translated into reliable, useful, and responsibly communicated information.
